About the data

Reviewed observations, with provenance kept intact.

This concept separates public scientific context from internal workflow complexity. The public sees approved observations and essential method context—not private collector information or an invented confidence score.

01

Approval is a publication boundary

Observations appear in the public product only after review. “Approved observation” and “Quality reviewed” describe workflow state; they are not claims that a water body is safe, healthy, or compliant.

02

Missing means missing

Measurements that were not collected remain absent. The interface does not silently replace missing values with zero or invent values between sampling events.

03

Dates are primary evidence

Collection date is shown prominently. When a specific observation is inspected, the collection time is presented in Eastern Time when available.

04

Thresholds need scientific context

This concept intentionally avoids generic red/yellow/green scoring. Regulatory or ecological thresholds should only appear when tied to a defensible parameter, jurisdiction, method, and purpose.

Prototype note

All values in this concept are synthetic.

The interface uses deterministic sample observations and representative Central Pennsylvania site geography solely to test information design, mapping, accessibility, and interaction behavior.

Monitoring program

Watershed Watch connects field collection to public understanding.

The public experience is designed around a simple scientific question: what was measured here, when, and after what review?

1Field collection

Monitoring observations are collected at known sites with measurement and date provenance.

2Validation & review

Submitted records pass through trusted validation and human quality review before publication.

3Approved publication

Only public-safe approved records cross the publication boundary.

4Public exploration

People can explore sites, measurements, and trends without seeing internal workflow complexity.
